ARRIVAL

Eighty-six billion neurons, and one quiet keyboard.

Everything you think passes through a body that has to type it. CogniDiff watches only the rhythm of that translation — never the words — and learns what normal looks like for you.

  • SPECIMEN HUMAN · CEREBRUM

THE BASELINE

Your baseline is you, not an average.

A fast typist and a slow typist are both perfectly normal. CogniDiff never compares you to a population — it builds a statistical picture of your own typing across two weeks, hour by hour, and measures everything after that against it.

  • REGION FRONTAL CORTEX
  • DEPTH 2.0 MM
  • WINDOW 14 DAYS

THE NETWORK

Rhythm, not words.

The scan disperses into what CogniDiff actually keeps: a constellation of timings. How long a key was held. The gap before the next one. How often you delete and retype. Your text never leaves the browser, because it is never read in the first place.

INSIGHT

A number is useless until it tells you why.

A score of 62 on its own invites either panic or dismissal. So every CogniScore arrives with the three things that moved it — your pauses are 21% longer, your corrections are up 14% — in words you can check against your own week.

  • REGION OCCIPITAL LOBE
  • METHOD SHAP · ABLATION
  • OUTPUT TOP 3 CHANGES

BALANCE

The quiet half that keeps you upright.

One bad night is not a signal. CogniDiff refuses to alarm on a single session — it escalates only on patterns that persist across days, and a low-confidence reading raises nothing at all.

  • REGION CEREBELLUM
  • NEURONS 69.8 B
  • FALSE POSITIVES <15%

SUMMARY

Detection of change. Never a diagnosis.

CogniDiff detects deviation from an individual's own typing baseline. It does not detect, diagnose or confirm cognitive decline, and no clinical sensitivity or specificity is claimed, because no clinically labelled data was used.
